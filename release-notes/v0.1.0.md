### 🪟 A web page as its own Mac app
- Each site in your config becomes its own app in `~/Applications`, with its own Dock icon, window and cookies, so signing in to one never touches another.
- Links stay in the app while they belong to the site's kept hosts and open in your browser otherwise. Downloads go to `~/Downloads`, and ⌘F finds text on the page.
- An app can open at login, and it reloads on its own when you change its site's config.

### 🔔 Notifications, badges and mail links
- A site's web notifications arrive as macOS notifications, under the app's own name.
- A pattern in the config reads a count out of the page's title and shows it on the Dock icon.
- A site can handle `mailto:` links, opening its compose page with the address filled in.

### 🔑 Sign in from 1Password
- Built-in sign-in flows for Google and Okta fill in the username, password and one-time code from 1Password, and pick the Okta verification method the login names.
- Sign in from the bar that appears on a sign-in page, from File › Sign In with 1Password (⇧⌘L), or with `berth signin`.
- Secrets go from 1Password straight to the running app over its own socket, and are never written to the config, a log or a command line.

### 🔏 Signed, installed with one command
- `berth sites build` copies and signs one app per site with Berth's own release certificate, so notification permission survives every rebuild.
- `berth update` installs the latest release and rebuilds every site app from it, and `berth doctor` checks the install, the config and each app.
- One release runs on macOS 15 or later, on Apple Silicon or Intel.
